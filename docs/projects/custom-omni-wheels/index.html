Custom Ball-Bearing Omni Wheels

Ball-bearing omni wheels for VEX to cut rotational friction, iterated from a fully custom clamshell wheel to a hybrid of stock VEX wheels with lightweight bearing adapters.

Mechanical DesignVEXRoboticsPrototyping

In VEX, teams usually run VEX’s own omni wheels. I wanted wheels that spin on ball bearings instead, to cut down rotational friction. Getting there took three versions and one side experiment, starting from a fully custom wheel and ending up somewhere much simpler.

Why ball bearings

Every bit of friction in the wheels is power the motors spend fighting the drivetrain instead of moving the robot. Ball bearings were my way of taking rotational friction out of the wheels themselves.

Version 1: fully custom

The first version was a fully custom clamshell wheel. It reused the ball bearings we already ran on our high-strength shafts, with an adapter so they fit the imperial screws we use.

It worked, but it had two real drawbacks. It was very heavy. And the gear was printed into the backplate, so changing the gear ratio meant printing a whole new backplate.

Version 2: modular and pocketed

The second version went after both problems. I made the gear a separate part, so gears could be swapped without a new backplate, and pocketed the wheel to bring the weight down. The trade-off was that attaching the gear now took more heat-set inserts.

Version 3: the hybrid

For the third version I stopped building the whole wheel. The hybrid uses VEX’s own wheels and adds a lightweight adapter with smaller bearings, so the bearings come along without the weight of a fully custom wheel.

A side experiment: replacing the housing

The last prototype I explored replaced the plastic housing of the VEX wheel entirely, so the bearings could sit natively in the housing instead of needing adapters. I didn’t go far down that route, but it proved the idea works: in principle we could swap out the housing and have bearings built into the wheel itself.

Where it landed

For now we’re running the hybrid design. Moving to custom housings would mean taking apart wheels to move their rollers over, and the adapters work fine as they are.
