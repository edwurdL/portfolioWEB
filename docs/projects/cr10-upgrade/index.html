CR-10 Controller & Firmware Overhaul

After a bad firmware flash bricked a Creality CR-10’s stock board, rebuilt it around a BTT SKR 3 with new stepper drivers, a TFT35 E3 touchscreen, and BLTouch auto bed leveling, running a custom Marlin build.

3D PrintingMarlinFirmwareElectronicsHardware

This started as a small upgrade and turned into a full electronics overhaul. The goal was to add a BLTouch auto bed leveling probe to a Creality CR-10. It ended with a new motherboard, new stepper drivers, a touchscreen, and a custom Marlin firmware build.

Bricking the stock board

The printer still had its stock CR-10 motherboard, and supporting a BLTouch meant flashing new firmware onto it. The wrong firmware went on. That board has no bootloader, so there was no easy way to recover it, and the printer was bricked.

The rebuild

Rather than try to revive the stock board, I used the setback as a chance to overhaul the electronics:

  • a BIGTREETECH SKR 3 motherboard in place of the stock board
  • new, quieter stepper drivers
  • a BIGTREETECH TFT35 E3 touchscreen in place of the stock knob controller
  • the BLTouch probe that started it all

Custom Marlin firmware

A new board in an old printer doesn’t run out of the box: the firmware has to describe the printer it’s driving. I configured my own Marlin build for this CR-10 on the SKR 3, then flashed it onto the board and adjusted it until everything worked together:

  • probe offsets and deployment for the BLTouch
  • mesh bed leveling
  • stepper driver and endstop setup
  • thermal settings

Testing

The rebuild was validated with leveling runs and first-layer test prints, repeated until the bed compensated correctly across its full area.
