Custom-Manufactured VEX Robot Structures

Laser-cut sheet plastic and metal structural parts for VEX competition robots, including a robot I designed and built, taken from an ABS prototype to a production version in polypropylene and aluminum.

Status
Completed
Date
Dec 2025 – May 2026
Built with
Mechanical Design, CAD, Laser Cutting, Manufacturing, VEX

On VEX competition robots, I replaced stock structural parts with custom ones laser-cut from sheet plastic and metal, trading weight against rigidity wherever a subsystem needed it. On our larger robot I was mainly responsible for the plastic guards. The smaller robot is the one I mainly designed and built, so most of this post follows it from its first prototype in December to where it stood by March.

The prototype: black plastic

The first version came together in early December. Its job was to test the geometry, and whether the robot could actually manipulate the game object the way the design intended. So I built it from materials that were quick to cut: the outer shell was 3 mm black ABS, and the internal ball guide was 1/16" ABS sheet. It was on the field two days after the parts were laid out.

Back to CAD

With the geometry proven, in late December I reworked the design in CAD into a production version, keeping the same layout but changing what it was made of.

Cutting the production parts

The production version kept the prototype’s dimensions and upgraded the materials:

  • Internal ball guide: 1/16" ABS became 1/16" aluminum. Same size, but much more rigid and stronger.
  • Outer shell: 3 mm black ABS became 4 mm white polypropylene. Polypropylene is less brittle than ABS, so it stands up better to impacts, and I thickened the walls around the pockets for the same reason.

I cut the new parts on January 10.

The final robot

The production version was assembled by January 11, a day after its plates were cut, and fully wired by January 19.

How I approached the parts

I picked the material and thickness of each part from the loads it carried. On the small robot that meant aluminum where the ball guide needed stiffness, and polypropylene where the shell had to survive impacts.

To save weight, I cut lightening pockets into low-stress regions. Parts that twisted under load got gussets and laminated layers instead.

The hole patterns follow the VEX grid, so the custom parts work with off-the-shelf hardware and could be swapped in mid-build.
